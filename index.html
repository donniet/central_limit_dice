Central Limit Dice

Roll N six-sided dice and add them together. Repeat many times and record each sum. A single die is flat (uniform), but the sum of several dice piles up in the middle — and the more dice you add, the closer the histogram gets to a normal curve. That's the Central Limit Theorem.

Things to try in class
  • N = 1: one die is uniform — every face is equally likely. The normal curve fits poorly.
  • N = 2: the classic triangle. 7 is most common because it has the most combinations.
  • N = 5 or more: the shape is already very close to normal, even though each die is uniform.
  • Compare the sample mean x̄ and SD s with the theoretical μ and σ. How many rolls until they're close?
  • Notice how the spread in absolute terms (σ) grows like √N, while the range (5N) grows like N — so relative to the full range, the distribution gets narrower as N increases.
